Why the Smithsonian Uses glTF for 3D Digitization

The Smithsonian Institution's Digitization Program Office is actively transforming millions of rare historical, scientific, and cultural artifacts into high-resolution 3D models. Central to this monumental effort is the adoption of glTF (GL Transmission Format), an open, royalty-free standard maintained by the Khronos Group. This article examines the significance of using glTF in the Smithsonian's digitization workflow, highlighting how the format solves critical challenges related to web performance, visual accuracy, open access, and long-term digital preservation for global cultural heritage.

The "JPEG of 3D" for Universal Web Delivery

Prior to the widespread adoption of modern 3D formats, sharing three-dimensional models online required heavy software plugins or proprietary viewers. glTF was engineered specifically to be the "JPEG of 3D"—an efficient, interoperable runtime asset format. By minimizing file sizes and streamlining data loading directly into the GPU via WebGL and WebGPU, glTF allows the Smithsonian to deliver complex historical objects to standard web browsers instantly, without lag or specialized hardware.

Long-Term Archival Safety Through Open Standards

National heritage institutions must avoid vendor lock-in and proprietary ecosystems that could become obsolete. glTF is an open, royalty-free format with extensive documentation and broad industry backing. Using glTF guarantees that the Smithsonian’s digital assets remain readable, editable, and stable for decades, protecting the public investment in digital curation and safeguarding global history against technological obsolescence.

Uncompromising Visual Fidelity with Physically Based Rendering

Cultural artifacts often feature complex surface properties, from the weathered bronze of an ancient statue to the woven texture of historical garments. glTF includes native support for Physically Based Rendering (PBR). This allows materials to react dynamically and accurately to lighting environments. As a result, users viewing digitized artifacts receive an experience that faithfully matches the real-world look and feel of the original museum pieces.

Democratizing Access and Educational Reuse

A primary goal of the Smithsonian Digitization project is open access. By standardizing on glTF, the institution makes it straightforward for educators, students, game developers, and researchers to download and integrate genuine historical assets into their own projects. glTF files can be readily imported into 3D printing software, game engines like Unreal Engine and Unity, and augmented reality (AR) or virtual reality (VR) environments on smartphones and headsets.

Catalyzing Cultural Heritage Standards Worldwide

When the world’s largest museum and research complex adopted glTF, it set a powerful benchmark for the broader cultural heritage sector. The Smithsonian’s implementation demonstrated to other museums, archives, and libraries that 3D assets can be shared at scale without sacrificing performance or visual fidelity. This shift has accelerated the adoption of open 3D standards across museums worldwide, making cultural preservation more collaborative and globally accessible.
