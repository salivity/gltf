glTF in the Open Metaverse and Avatars

glTF (GL Transmission Format) serves as the foundational 3D asset standard enabling true interoperability and asset portability across the open-source metaverse. Often dubbed the "JPEG of 3D," this royalty-free format developed by the Khronos Group provides a lightweight, efficient runtime delivery mechanism for 3D content. By minimizing both the file size of 3D assets and the runtime processing required to unpack and render them, glTF removes proprietary friction, empowering users and developers to move virtual spaces and humanoid avatars across disparate engines, virtual worlds, and web applications seamlessly.

The Standard for Runtime Efficiency

Unlike authoring formats such as FBX or USD, which are designed for editing and contain heavy production metadata, glTF is purpose-built for efficient transmission and fast loading. It packs scene hierarchies, geometry, animations, and physically based rendering (PBR) materials into a compact JSON structure alongside binary payloads (.glb). For the open-source metaverse—which relies heavily on browser-based engines utilizing WebGL and WebGPU—glTF allows 3D environments and assets to load instantly over standard HTTP connections without requiring proprietary runtimes or intensive conversions.

Driving Avatar Interoperability via VRM

The most critical impact of glTF on digital identity is its role as the base architecture for VRM, the leading open standard for 3D humanoid avatars. Built directly on top of glTF 2.0 as an extension, VRM standardizes humanoid bone structures, blend shapes for facial expressions, first-person camera viewpoints, physics-based bone dynamics (such as hair and clothing movement), and avatar-specific usage rights.

Because VRM is an extension of glTF, any platform that supports glTF can easily integrate VRM models. This enables a user to carry a single digital identity across independent virtual worlds, social VR platforms, streaming setups, and Web3 environments, breaking down the walled gardens of centralized virtual ecosystems.

Engine-Agnostic Cross-Platform Portability

A core pillar of an open metaverse is neutrality regarding the underlying technology stack. glTF is supported natively or via robust plugins across major game engines (Unreal Engine, Unity, Godot) and web-first frameworks (Three.js, Babylon.js, A-Frame).

This universal compatibility allows open-source metaverse platforms such as Mozilla Hubs, Vircadia, and Hyperfy to interact with the same asset repositories. Digital assets created in open-source tools like Blender can be exported directly to glTF and deployed across any of these environments with consistent geometry, textures, and skeletal animations.

Extensibility for Future Metaverse Needs

glTF's modular architecture relies on extensions, allowing the format to evolve alongside emerging virtual reality and metaverse requirements without breaking backward compatibility. Through official Khronos extensions, glTF supports advanced material properties (such as transmission, clearcoat, and sheen), audio integration, and collision data. Open-source initiatives continue to draft extensions for interactive behaviors, metadata provenance, and decentralized storage integration.

By establishing an open, lightweight, and universally adopted baseline for 3D objects and digital identity, glTF eliminates platform vendor lock-in and forms the technical backbone of an interconnected, user-owned metaverse.
