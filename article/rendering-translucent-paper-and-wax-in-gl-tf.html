Rendering Translucent Paper and Wax in glTF

This article examines how to visually differentiate translucent paper and wax within 3D scenes by utilizing specific glTF PBR extensions. While both materials allow light to pass through them, their visual properties diverge sharply: paper behaves as a thin, highly matte diffuser, whereas wax exhibits depth, volumetric light absorption, and a soft specular sheen. By targeting extensions such as KHR_materials_diffuse_transmission, KHR_materials_volume, and core PBR roughness controls, artists and developers can accurately capture these distinct physical behaviors.

Fundamental Visual Distinctions

Translucent paper and wax interact with light in fundamentally different ways:

  • Paper (Thin-Surface Scattering): Paper is extremely thin and composed of compressed fibers. It exhibits immediate diffuse transmission—light entering one side scatters almost instantly and exits the opposite side evenly across the surface. It lacks noticeable volumetric depth, has a high micro-surface roughness that eliminates sharp reflections, and casts relatively crisp shadows when backlit.
  • Wax (Dense Volumetric Scattering): Wax is an organic solid with significant physical depth. Light enters the material, penetrates beneath the surface, bounces internally, and is absorbed over distance before re-emerging. This produces soft, glowing edges, blurred self-shadows, and a warm internal color shift. Unlike paper, wax typically has a smooth or semi-glossy surface that produces noticeable specular highlights.

glTF Extensions for Translucent Paper

To model thin translucent paper, the scene must simulate light passing through a surface without computing internal geometry thickness.

KHR_materials_diffuse_transmission

This extension is the primary tool for thin translucent objects. It allows incoming light to scatter diffusely through the reverse side of a mesh.

  • diffuseTransmissionFactor: Set close to 1.0 (or modulated with a texture) to allow backlighting to illuminate the paper sheet.
  • diffuseTransmissionColorFactor: Kept neutral or slightly warm to reflect the paper pulp's tint.

Core PBR Parameters for Paper

  • roughnessFactor: Set high, generally between 0.8 and 1.0. Paper does not produce clear reflections; light hitting the surface scatters diffusely.
  • metallicFactor: Set strictly to 0.0.
  • Volume Settings: Omit KHR_materials_volume. Paper is modeled as a single-sided or thin-walled geometry where volumetric calculations are physically unnecessary and computationally wasteful.

glTF Extensions for Wax

Recreating wax requires combining surface diffuse transmission with volumetric absorption to convey physical mass and internal light decay.

KHR_materials_volume

Wax relies on this extension to define how light attenuates as it travels through the interior of the 3D mesh.

  • thicknessFactor: Represents the actual thickness of the object along its surface normals. A texture map can modulate thin areas (such as the rim of a candle) versus thick areas.
  • attenuationDistance: Defines the average distance light travels before being absorbed. For wax, this value should be short to medium (e.g., a few millimeters to centimeters), ensuring only shallow areas glow brightly.
  • attenuationColor: Set to the internal color of the wax (typically warm yellow, amber, or deep red). As white light penetrates the wax, other wavelengths are absorbed, producing a saturated glow inside the material.

KHR_materials_diffuse_transmission

When paired with KHR_materials_volume, diffuse transmission allows light to exit the wax diffusely rather than behaving like clear glass. The factor determines how much light breaks out of the volume across the object's surface.

Core PBR and Optical Properties for Wax

  • roughnessFactor: Set between 0.2 and 0.5. Unpolished or paraffin wax retains a soft, satin specular reflection rather than paper's flat finish.
  • KHR_materials_ior: Paraffin and beeswax have an Index of Refraction (IOR) around 1.43 to 1.46. Applying this extension ensures accurate Fresnel falloff at grazing angles.
