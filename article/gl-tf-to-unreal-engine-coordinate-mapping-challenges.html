glTF to Unreal Engine Coordinate Mapping Challenges

Importing glTF 3D models into Unreal Engine introduces technical hurdles driven by conflicting spatial conventions. glTF relies on a right-handed, Y-up coordinate system standardizing units in meters, whereas Unreal Engine operates on a left-handed, Z-up system using centimeters. Bridging this gap requires complex transformations that impact mesh geometry, winding order, bone hierarchies, and physics interactions.

Handedness and Axis Swapping

The primary geometric conflict between glTF and Unreal Engine lies in their axis alignment:

  • glTF: Right-handed with +X pointing right, +Y pointing up, and +Z pointing toward the viewer (-Z points forward).
  • Unreal Engine: Left-handed with +X pointing forward, +Y pointing right, and +Z pointing up.

To align the spaces, an importer cannot simply rotate the model; it must invert an axis to change the coordinate handedness. This conversion typically requires negating or swapping axes (for example, mapping glTF’s \(+Y\) to Unreal’s \(+Z\), and glTF’s \(+Z\) to Unreal’s \(-X\)).

Polygon Winding Order and Normal Inversion

Changing coordinate handedness flips the geometric winding order of polygon vertices. In computer graphics, clockwise versus counter-clockwise vertex definitions determine whether a triangle faces toward or away from the camera.

If an axis reflection occurs without reversing the polygon triangle indices, backface culling treats front-facing polygons as backward. This causes meshes to render inside-out or disappear entirely. Along with vertex winding, tangent vectors and bitangent signs (stored in the glTF mesh to define surface curvature) must be inverted or recalculated to prevent inverted shading in normal maps.

Unit Scaling Discrepancies

glTF defines all spatial measurements strictly in meters, while Unreal Engine standardizes scene coordinates in centimeters.

Resolving this requires a uniform 100x scale factor applied during import. While static meshes easily absorb this scale factor into their raw vertex data, problems arise when:

  • Transforms are dynamically driven at runtime.
  • Root motion animations carry unit values designed for meters into an environment expecting centimeters.
  • Physics collision hulls and mass properties become distorted if the scale is applied to parent components rather than the raw asset data.

Skeletal Rigs and Joint Orientations

Mapping skeletal structures between the two systems is among the most prone to errors. glTF stores skinning transforms as local translations, rotations (quaternions), and scales relative to parent joints.

When converting a skeleton from right-handed Y-up to left-handed Z-up:

  1. Rest Poses and Bind Matrices: The inverse bind matrices must be recomputed in Unreal's space to prevent mesh distortion during deformation.
  2. Joint Rotation Limits: Unreal Engine assigns specific rotational axes to bones for retargeting and inverse kinematics (IK). glTF rigs often import with rotated bone axes, leading to unpredictable joint twisting or broken procedural IK solvers.
  3. Quaternion Inversion: Negating or swapping axes demands matching mathematical adjustments to animation curve quaternions. An improper quaternion sign flip can introduce unexpected 180-degree rotations between animation keyframes.

Camera and Directional Light Alignments

glTF defines directional lights and cameras looking along the negative Z-axis (\(-Z\)), with up defined as \(+Y\). Unreal Engine expects cameras and lights to emit along the positive X-axis (\(+X\)), with up defined as \(+Z\).

If conversion matrices do not include local corrective offsets for these non-mesh elements, imported scene lights illuminate opposite directions and imported cinematic cameras point away from the intended target.
