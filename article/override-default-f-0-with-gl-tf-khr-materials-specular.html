Override Default F0 with glTF KHR_materials_specular

This article explains how the glTF extension KHR_materials_specular enables 3D artists to override the fixed baseline specular reflectance (\(F_0\)) used in standard glTF PBR workflows. In standard glTF 2.0, non-metallic (dielectric) surfaces are hardcoded to a default 4% reflectance value, limiting material fidelity. By introducing configurable color and intensity parameters, alongside texture maps, KHR_materials_specular allows fine-grained control to scale, tint, or completely mask specular highlights on dielectric surfaces.

The Default glTF Specular Limitation

In the standard glTF 2.0 metallic-roughness model, dielectric materials lack direct specular configuration. Their specular reflectance at normal incidence (\(F_0\)) is hardcoded to 0.04 (4% linear reflectance), which approximates an index of refraction (IOR) of 1.5. While this represents many everyday materials like plastics, wood, and stone accurately, it fails for materials with noticeably lower reflectance (such as water at ~2%) or significantly higher reflectance (such as gemstones, which can exceed 15%).

Parameters Introduced by KHR_materials_specular

The KHR_materials_specular extension solves this limitation by introducing four key properties that modulate the dielectric \(F_0\):

  • specularFactor: A floating-point scalar (default 1.0) that scales the overall strength of the specular reflection.
  • specularTexture: A texture where the alpha channel (A) acts as a per-pixel multiplier for the specular intensity.
  • specularColorFactor: A 3D vector (RGB, default [1.0, 1.0, 1.0]) that tints the specular reflection.
  • specularColorTexture: An sRGB texture whose color components tint the specular highlight across the surface.

How the Extension Overrides F0

Rather than replacing the core metallic-roughness model, KHR_materials_specular acts as a scaling and tinting layer on top of dielectric reflectance calculations.

Under standard PBR, dielectric normal-incidence reflectance is derived from the material's index of refraction (which defaults to 1.5 unless modified by the KHR_materials_ior extension):

\[F_0^{\text{dielectric}} = \left(\frac{\text{IOR} - 1}{\text{IOR} + 1}\right)^2\]

When KHR_materials_specular is active, the engine recalculates the dielectric \(F_0\) using the following formula:

\[F_0 = \text{min}\left(F_0^{\text{dielectric}} \times \text{specularColorFactor} \times \text{specularColorTexture.rgb}, \, 1.0\right) \times \text{specularFactor} \times \text{specularTexture.a}\]

By altering these values, artists can modify the final \(F_0\) in several ways:

  1. Reducing or Removing Specular Highlights: Setting specularFactor below 1.0 (or driving it toward 0.0 via specularTexture) reduces the dielectric \(F_0\) below 0.04. This enables realistic rendering of low-IOR surfaces like water, ice, or deep cavities where micro-occlusion eliminates specular highlights.
  2. Amplifying Reflectance: In conjunction with KHR_materials_ior, setting a higher IOR shifts the baseline \(F_0\) upward, which the specular parameters can then selectively modulate.
  3. Colored Dielectric Specular: Standard dielectrics produce white specular reflections. specularColorFactor and specularColorTexture allow custom RGB values to be multiplied into \(F_0\), enabling complex optical phenomena such as iridescent films, custom coated materials, and stylized art directions without falsely flagging the material as metallic.

Through these controls, KHR_materials_specular gives artists the ability to author physically accurate surface variations that break free from glTF's default 4% dielectric reflectance constraint.
